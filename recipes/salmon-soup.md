# Salmon Soup

When I was in Finland I had one of the best bowls of soup in my life. After some research I managed to recreate it in the US. Here's the result. Credit to the Finnish Salmon Soup Recpie from "Cooking the Globe.com" for giving me ideas to try. Their recipe is here: https://cookingtheglobe.com/finnish-salmon-soup-lohikeitto/

Takes about 15 minutes to prep, and 45 minutes cooking time.

## Ingredients

* 1 leek
* 1/4 c unsalted butter and 3/4 c heavy cream OR 1 c lactose free whole milk and 1/2 c butter
* 5 cups of low sodium vegetable broth
* 2 carrots around 155 grams
* 1 lb of waxy boiling potatoes (not baking or russet potatoes, they are too flaky in the liquid)
* 1lb (450g) of a combination of smoked trout and cold smoked salmon. For example I recently used 186g of smoked trout and made up the rest with whole foods brand cold smoked atlantic salmon
* fresh dill and/or dried dill or dillweed
* black pepper
* salt


## Instructions


* Cut the white end of **1 leek** off from the green part, then cut in half length-wise, rinse out any grit or dirt in the folds under running water, then thinly slice each half the short direction, making half moon crescents.
* In a heavy-bottom large pot heated to medium heat, heat up **1/4 cup of unsalted butter** and add the leek to the bottom of the pot in a single layer. Cook until the leek is translucent, about 3-5 minutes.
* Pour in **64 fluid ounces of vegetable broth** and bring to a boil.
* Add to the boiling pot the following ingredients that have been cut at least in half, and preferably chopped roughly into large, almost choking-hazard sized pieces: **1 carrot**, **1 lb of waxy red or yellow boiling potatoes (not russet or baking potatoes)**. Cook about 20-30 minutes or until a fork effortlessly cuts through the potato as if it were a stick of cool butter.
* Bring the pot from a boil to a low simmer and add to it **1 smoked trout both filets** as well as at least **1 lb of cold smoked salmon**. Make sure the fish has been chopped or broken up into bite-sized pieces.
* Stir into the pot **3/4 c of heavy cream.**
* Keep on low heat until the fish and cream are heated through, just a couple of minutes.
* Liberously add **cracked ground black pepper**, **pinches of salt** and **fresh chopped dill** to the pot to taste, and serve immediately.


## Tips, Tricks, Variations

* One time I only had 4 cups of broth so I added water to get to 5 cups. Nobody seemed to mind.
* I'll typically cube the carrots the match the size of the potato cubes, typically around 0.5-1 inch in size, with the potato cubes slightly larger. I typically do not peel either carrots or potatoes, I just wash them well.
* The cream, fish, and vegetable broth tend to have unpredictable amounts of sodium in them, plus depending on the altitude you cook this and how much the liquid distills down, that will also change the salt content. Therefore, I strongly recommend using unsalted butter instead of salted butter at the beginning, and also waiting until the very end to add any more salt to taste. 
* For whatever reason I find the Swanson's or the generic brands of the cheapest vegetable broth available at the grocery store to be much tastier than others. The original recipes called for using fish broth but I found the vegetable broth much better in taste plus more accessible and easier to manage than either homemade or storebought fish broth. I've also made vegetable broth from scratch, or done a double vegetable broth, and they are delicious. Typically because of where I get the fish, I'll pick up the Whole Foods brand organic vegetable low sodium broth.
* One time I didn't have heavy cream only regular milk, and I got away with it by doubling the butter to **1/2 cup of butter** and replacing the heavy cream with **1/2 c of milk** and it worked just fine. 
* Whole foods seems to be the best place for the fish in this recipe. I get their cold smoked trout that they sell, and also their largest store name brand of cold smoked atlantic salmon and throw that in. I'm sure others would work just as well, but the goal is to add smokiness to the soup without haing overcooked fish.
* This soup is super delicious with slices of rustic or sourdough bread or toast with a touch of butter.
* The fresh dill feels fancier and is fun to chop and sprinkle liberally, but I feel that the dry dill or **dill weed** gives a stronger flavor of the herb, and I don't believe it's possible to have too much dill in this recipe.
