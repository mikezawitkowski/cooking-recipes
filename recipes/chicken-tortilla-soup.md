# Chicken Tortilla Soup

In my 20s I purchased my first cookbook, "The Art of Simple Food" by Alice Waters. And one of the first receipes I cooked from it was Chicken Tortilla Soup.

At the time, I knew almost nothing cooking food. This book helped get me started. I probably took Waters' words too seriously, and followed recipes too closely, afraid to deviate one bit from the commanding words of a leading culinary authority. 

The good news is that eons later I'm much better at not following instructions to the letter, and my recipes are better for it. 

These changes are for baking at about 5600 feet in elevation.



## Ingredients

* 1.5-2 quarts chicken broth (2 quarts = 8 cups)
* 1 chicken breast half (with skin and bones for best flavor)
* 1/2 cup peanut or vegetable oil
* 4 corn tortillas cut into 1/2 inch strips
* 2 tablespoons of olive oil
* 1 anaheim green pepper, seeded and thinly sliced
* 2 garlic cloves, thinly sliced
* 1/2 medium yellow onion, thinly sliced
* 2 tomatoes, peeled, seeded, and diced; or 3 small cannced whole tomatoes, diced (juice included)
* 1 dried chipotle chile, seeds removed
* a LOT of salt, measured with your heart
* can of black beans (optional)
* frozen corn (optional)

### Optional Garnishes

* 1/2 cup chopped cilantro
* 6 lime wedges
* 4 ounces crumbled queso fresco or grated Monterey Jack cheese
* 1/2 cup peeled and diced jicama
* 1/2 cup diced radish
* 1 cubed avocado

### Other Variations

* can of black beans

## Instructions

### Act One

* Bring the broth to a boil briefly than reduce to simmer
* Add to broth raw chicken breast and cook at a bare simmer until chicken is fully cooked and temp is 165 degrees F. This typically takes about 20 minutes.
* Turn off the heat, then use tongs to or a fork to transfer the breast(s) to a plate to cool.

### Act Two

* If you're like me you only have one big pot, pour the broth into another container, no need to wash but use heat and paper towels to remove any moisture from where the oil is going to be added at the bottom
* In that large heavy pot, heat 2 T of olive oil, then add 1 anaheim green pepper, seeded and thinly sliced, 1/2 medium yellow onion, thinly sliced, and garlic cloves and salt. Cook that until it is soft, about 5 minutes.
* Next pour in the hot broth, then add 2 tomatoes, peeled seeded and diced; or 3 small canned whole tomatoes, diced (juice included); add 1 dried chipotle chile, seeds removed; add salt
* (Optional: If you want to add frozen corn kernels, now is a good time for that)
* Bring the mixture to a boil, and then turn down to a simmer and cook for 30 minutes

### Act Three

While the soup cooks for 30 minutes, let's make the tortilla strips.

* While the soup simmers, in an 8" heavy bottom skillet over medium heat, pour in 1/2 c peanut or vegetable oil
* Add 4 tortillas cut into strips
* fry in small batches until golden brown and crispy
* let rest on paper towels and season with salt


### Act Four

* At about 25 minutes into the simmering of the sopu, the chicken should be cool enough, so while the rest cooks, remove bones and skin, then shred meat into bite sized pieces for later
* This is a good time to prep your garnishes


## Tips, Tricks, Variations

* My first years of cooking this recipe led to complaints from my partner that it was always delicious but too oily. I'm embarrassed to admit how long it took me to consider deviating from the recipe to fix this.  Seriously, there's no shame in straining the broth through a strainer or colander to temporarily remove the larger ingredients, then pour the broth through combinations of a "fat separator" (looks like a tea kettle measuring cup with the spout on the bottom), and cheese cloth. Then you can selectively add back the larger chunks of ingredients. In the end, nobody has to know, they'll just focus on how great it tastes. A good book that goes more into depth on this is "Sauces" by James Peterson.
* Something I've done in the past that makes an even richer, tastier broth is to make a broth from scratch, simmering ingredients for half the day in a giant pot. However, instead of using water I use chicken broth. Then I take that "double broth" and repeat the process again to make a "triple broth" and that is what is used for the soup.
* I've replaced the 1/2 median yellow onion with 1/2 large white onion and it still tastes great.
* 4 yellow tortillas cut into strips and friend and salted in 350 degree vegetable oil
* One time instead of peppers mentioned in the original recipe I used 4 shishito peppers thinly sliced, keeping the seeds in since they are quite mild
* I've been known to increase the simmer of the raw chicken breast in broth to rush to get the chicken to 165 degrees F sooner if in a hurry
* You can add black beans to make a more substantial soup. I've added them after cooking separately, because it doesn't muck up the broth as much as if you were to add them in the hot broth and cook them
* I've extended the soup on future days by adding additional ingredients and broth
* Almost every time I burn my fingers shredding the chicken because I don't wait long enough for it to cool. Try to wait until the last five minutes of the 30 minute soup cook time to add it and save yourself the literal pain.
